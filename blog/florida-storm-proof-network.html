Storm-Proof Your Network: Battery Backup, Surge Protection & 5G Failover

By Jorge Bello, founder · Nov 23, 2026 · 8 min read

In Central Florida, the threat to your network usually isn't hackers. It's the weather. Afternoon thunderstorms, lightning, hurricanes and the brief power flickers that come with them knock networks offline, reboot cameras right when you need them, and fry modems and switches every summer.

The good news: a few inexpensive pieces of equipment and some settings keep your Wi-Fi, cameras and internet running through most of it. Here's what we recommend for homes and small businesses.

What Actually Takes Networks Down

  • Power flickers. Even a one-second outage reboots your modem, router, switch and access points. Getting everything back online can take several minutes, and cameras stop recording in the meantime.
  • Surges and lightning. Florida sees more lightning than almost any other state. A nearby strike can send a surge through power lines, coax or long Ethernet runs and damage equipment.
  • Internet outages. During storms, your internet provider's own equipment in the neighborhood can lose power or be damaged, even if your house has power.
  • Long outages. After a hurricane, power can be out for days.

Each one has a different fix.

1. Put the Core Network on a UPS

A UPS (uninterruptible power supply) is a battery that sits between the wall and your equipment. When power flickers, the battery takes over instantly, and nothing reboots. This is the single most useful thing you can add.

  • What to plug in: the modem or fiber box (ONT), your UniFi gateway, and your main PoE switch. The switch matters because it powers your access points and cameras.
  • How big: most homes and small offices are fine with a 600–1500 VA unit. A typical modem, gateway and small PoE switch draw well under the rating of these units, and many give 20 minutes or more of runtime. More cameras and access points mean more PoE power and less runtime, so size for your actual load.
  • Type: a line-interactive UPS with a pure sine wave output is a good choice for network gear. Avoid cheap units without automatic voltage regulation.
  • Replace the battery every 3–5 years. Most UPS units have a self-test; if it fails, the battery is done.

Ubiquiti also makes rack-mount and tower UPS units, and a switch with a built-in battery, which report their status right in UniFi.

2. Protect Against Surges and Lightning

  • Choose a UPS with surge protection (most have it) so power surges are absorbed before they reach your gear.
  • Consider a whole-home surge protector. An electrician installs it at your electrical panel. It protects everything in the house, and it's a common recommendation in Florida.
  • Protect outdoor cable runs. Outdoor cameras and access points connect with Ethernet cable that can carry a surge from a nearby strike straight into your switch. Ethernet surge protectors, properly grounded, at the point where the cable enters the building help prevent that. Use outdoor-rated cable outside.
  • Coax counts too. If your internet comes over coax cable, it should be grounded where it enters the house. Your internet provider can check this.

3. Add Backup Internet

A UPS keeps your network on, but it can't help if your internet provider's line is down. A backup connection can:

  • UniFi 5G or LTE backup: a small cellular device that connects to your UniFi gateway. When the main line fails, the gateway switches to cellular automatically, and switches back when the main line returns. You'll need a data plan for it; our unlimited 5G data SIMs include a public IP in most locations, so remote access keeps working during failover.
  • A second wired provider: for businesses, a second internet line from a different provider (for example, fiber plus cable) on the gateway's second WAN port.
  • Starlink: another option in areas with limited choices, on a second WAN port.

In UniFi, set the second connection to failover (not load balancing) in Settings → Internet if it's a metered cellular plan, so you only use cellular data when the main line is down. Put the backup device on the UPS too, or it goes down with the power.

4. Prepare for Long Outages

  • Generator: a UPS bridges the gap while a generator starts. Without a UPS, everything reboots when power switches over.
  • Back up your UniFi configuration. UniFi consoles can back up to the cloud automatically. Make sure it's turned on, so if storm damage takes out your gateway, a replacement can be restored in minutes.
  • Get notified. Turn on notifications in the UniFi app so you know when devices go offline or come back while you're away.
  • Keep cameras recording. Your camera recorder, PoE switch and cameras all need to stay powered to keep recording during an outage. See our UniFi Protect setup guide.

Before a Storm: Quick Checklist

  1. Make sure every UPS is charged and passes its self-test.
  2. Check that UniFi cloud backups are recent.
  3. Confirm your cellular backup has an active data plan.
  4. If you're evacuating and a direct lightning strike is a real concern, unplugging the modem's coax and the power to network gear is the only full protection.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will a UPS keep my internet running?

It depends on the battery size and what's plugged in. A modem, gateway and small switch on a mid-size UPS often run 20–60 minutes, enough to cover most flickers and short outages. Check the UPS manufacturer's runtime chart for your load.

Will my internet work during a power outage if I have a UPS?

Your own equipment will stay on, but your internet provider's equipment in the neighborhood may lose power too. That's where a cellular backup helps.

Is a surge protector strip enough?

It's better than nothing, but a UPS with surge protection, plus a whole-home protector, gives much better protection, especially for long cable runs to outdoor cameras and access points.

Do I need all of this for a home network?

Most homes get the biggest benefit from a single UPS on the modem, router and switch. Backup internet matters most if you work from home or rely on cameras.
