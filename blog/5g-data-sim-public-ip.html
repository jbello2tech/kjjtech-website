Why a Public IP Matters

With CGNAT, your router shares one public address with many other customers. You can browse and stream fine, but the connection only works outbound. Nothing can connect in: you can't host a VPN server, reach your cameras directly, or forward a port.

With a public IP, a 5G connection behaves like a real internet line. That opens up setups that don't work on a regular cellular plan:

  • 5G as your main internet at a second location. A cabin, RV, jobsite, construction trailer or small shop with no cable or fiber can run a UniFi 5G Max (an outdoor one on the roof or a pole if the signal inside is weak) and still host a WireGuard VPN server, so you can reach the cameras and devices there from anywhere.
  • Remote access that survives a failover. If your UniFi gateway fails over to 5G backup when the main line goes down, a public IP on the backup means your VPN and remote access keep working. See our storm-ready network guide for setting up failover.
  • Site-to-site VPNs between a 5G location and your office or home.

On the road, a Mudi 7 is the VPN client, connecting out to your home server, so it doesn't need a public IP. There, the benefit is the unlimited data: your whole tunnel home, every laptop, phone and streaming stick, runs on one plan without hitting a hotspot cap.

A standard public IP can change from time to time. If you're hosting something on it, use dynamic DNS (UniFi has it built in) or add a static IP.

Add a Static IP

For a few dollars a month, you can add a static IP: a public address assigned to your SIM that doesn't change. It's worth it when:

  • Something needs to find you at a fixed address: a VPN server, a site-to-site tunnel, or remote access to a camera recorder, without relying on dynamic DNS.
  • You need to be on an allowlist. Business apps, cloud firewalls, client portals and some banking systems only accept connections from approved IPs.
  • You're routing traffic through the 5G line, for example sending inbound connections or specific services over it from your UniFi gateway with port forwarding and policy-based routes.

The static IP works by switching the router's cellular APN to a dedicated one. We set that up for you, on the UniFi 5G Max, GL.iNet routers and Peplink.

UniFi 5G Max (Indoor and Outdoor): Fixed Locations and Backup

Ubiquiti's 5G modem for UniFi networks comes in two versions. The indoor UniFi 5G Max sits on a desk or wall near a window. The UniFi 5G Max Outdoor is weatherproof, with directional antennas, and mounts on a roof, wall or pole to pull in a stronger signal at rural sites, jobsites and buildings with poor reception indoors. Both take a nano-SIM and are powered over a single network cable (PoE). Use either one as the main internet at a 5G-only site, or as automatic backup internet on your UniFi gateway. It shows up in UniFi alongside the rest of your network, and with a public or static IP, your UniFi VPN server, Teleport and camera access keep working over 5G.

The SIMs also work in most other unlocked 5G routers and hotspots that take a nano-SIM, including other GL.iNet and Peplink models. If you're not sure about yours, ask.
